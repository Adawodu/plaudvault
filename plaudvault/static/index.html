How this works

Your Plaud device uploads recordings to Plaud's cloud — on pin models there is no way around that, since they have no USB mode. Everything after that happens here: the audio is pulled down to your own disk, transcribed and summarized on your own machine, and then you decide what it becomes.

Plaud charges for transcription minutes, not storage. Because their AI never touches your audio, those minutes stay unspent.

The daily loop

  1. Sync — press Sync now, or let the schedule do it. Downloads, transcribes, summarizes, writes notes, and proposes actions.
  2. Triage the Inbox — open each recording and decide where it belongs.
  3. Work the Actions board — accept what's real, complete it, score the outcome.
  4. Check Measures — weekly, to see whether any of this is actually paying off.

Moving between recordings

Open a recording and ← prev / next → step through whatever list you came from — the Inbox steps through untriaged recordings, search results step through the hits, Trends steps through the chart in time order. The ← and → arrow keys do the same, except while you are typing or when the audio player has focus, where the arrows scrub the audio instead.

The list is captured when you open the first recording and does not change underneath you. Triaging something removes it from the live Inbox query, so a list that recomputed as you worked would shift you forward and silently skip the next one.

Triage: the three tiers

Into cognitive stack — the transcript is copied into your stack/ folder, the only directory your knowledge index reads. Use this for work you want searchable and connected.

Local only — archived and searchable here, but kept out of the corpus. The right default for anything personal or involving other people. A pin recorder picks up whoever is in earshot; they did not opt in.

Exclude — noise, misfires, empty rooms. Same as pressing dismiss on a card.

Dismissing takes a recording out of the Inbox, Library, Search, Trends and stack/, and stops the pipeline spending model time on it. Nothing is deleted — the audio and its verification facts stay on your drive, so it remains verifiable and prunable later. And because triage lives in its own table that sync never writes to, a dismissal survives every future sync rather than reappearing.

Tick show dismissed in the Library to review or restore them. The Library always says how many are hidden — quiet by default should never mean silently missing.

Tiering is physical, not advisory: changing a recording to local-only deletes its copy from stack/ on the next sync.

Actions

Each recording is scanned for commitment — things a person actually said they would do. They arrive as proposed and do nothing until you accept them. Many recordings contain nothing actionable — an empty list is a normal, correct answer, not a failure.

suggestion — implied next steps — is off by default. A local model is bad at that judgment: on this archive it produced 198 suggestions against 57 commitments, mostly topic summaries rather than tasks. Turn it on with plaudctl extract --suggestions if you want them.

Every proposal carries the transcript line it came from, and that line is verified against the recording before the action reaches this board. A quote that can't be traced is discarded — the quote is how you check the action, so an invented one is worse than no action at all.

Accepting asks for an intent: what this is supposed to achieve. That is what the outcome score is later judged against. Finishing a task and the task having worked are different things, and only one of them is worth measuring.

A commitment that keeps recurring can be promoted to a system under the Systems tab, which then tracks an adherence rate over time.

Search

The Search tab matches meaning, not wording. Ask for "feeling underpaid" and it finds "they went below the range that I gave" — no shared words. Each hit opens the recording cued to the moment it was said.

Every score is raw cosine similarity, not a confidence. There is no number below which a result is wrong — most unrelated English sits around 0.3–0.5, so a best hit at 0.55 may still be the closest thing you have. Compare hits to each other and expect quality to drop after the first few.

Embeddings always run locally through Ollama, even if your summary model is a hosted API — indexing sends every sentence you have ever recorded, and that shouldn't quietly follow a setting made for summarizing one file. Excluded recordings are left out.

Tone and the trend

Every transcript is scored for emotional register as part of the normal run — no switch to flip. Each is read in segments and reduced, so a long conversation that turned partway through registers as mixed instead of averaging out flat. Two numbers come back:

Valence — how positive or negative it felt, from −1 to +1. Energy — how activated, from 0 to 1, independently of valence. An argument and a celebration are both high energy.

A third number matters more than either: confidence. This is a language model reading a machine transcript, which has no tone of voice in it, drops words and mangles names. When the model says it isn't sure, the console draws that reading as a hollow dot and leaves it out of the trend line. Nothing here is a measurement of how anyone felt — it is a prompt to go back and listen.

Most conversation is unremarkable, and readings near zero are the common, correct answer. The value is in the Trends tab, where a year of them says something a single recording never could. Click any point to open that recording.

Recordings you tier exclude are left off the chart, just as they are kept out of stack/ — otherwise the demo files that shipped with the device, and your own misfires, sit in the trend reporting their mood as yours. A checkbox above the chart folds them back in.

Is the vault up to date?

The pill in the header answers it at a glance, and opens the full breakdown. "Up to date" is not one fact — it fails in several independent ways, and this checks all of them:

  • recordings sitting in Plaud's cloud that never reached this disk
  • audio downloaded but never transcribed, summarized, scored or scanned
  • notes the manifest records that no longer exist in your vault
  • a stack/ corpus that has drifted from your triage decisions

Untriaged recordings and unreviewed proposals are listed separately and never count against the verdict — the machine has done its part, and an indicator that turns amber because you have reading to do is one you'll learn to ignore.

The cloud check costs a network call, so it happens when you ask, not on a timer: open Vault status and press Check Plaud now, or run plaudctl fresh --cloud.

Deleting from Plaud's cloud

Open a recording and tick mark for cloud deletion. Nothing is deleted by any scheduled job — marking only makes it eligible. You then run:

plaudctl prune            # dry run, shows what would go
plaudctl prune --yes      # actually sends it

The first time, you must verify the deletion endpoint on a single recording with plaudctl prune --probe --yes — Plaud's delete API is undocumented, so this proves it behaves before anything else is touched. Deletion uses Plaud's trash, so it stays recoverable in their app for a window.

A recording is only eligible if its download was verified complete, its local copy still hashes clean, it has been transcribed, and it is older than the configured floor.

Command line

plaudctl run          sync → transcribe → summarize → sentiment → notes → extract → index
plaudctl search "…"   semantic search across every transcript
plaudctl index        embed transcripts for search
plaudctl fresh        is the vault up to date? (--cloud also asks Plaud)
plaudctl sentiment    score tone for anything not yet scored
plaudctl status       what is archived and what is pending
plaudctl verify       re-hash the archive, catch bitrot or missing files
plaudctl web          this console
plaudctl service install    run automatically in the background
plaudctl init         reconfigure storage, transcription, or model
